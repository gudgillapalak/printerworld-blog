Laptop Buying Guide for Small Businesses

Choose business laptops with the right performance, security, durability, connectivity, warranty and lifecycle cost for your small business.

Buying guides·8 min read·Guide 7 of 50

Introduction

A laptop is an essential piece of kit for any small business. With the ability to interact with your customers, manage accounts, create documents, attend meetings, run business software and store important information. If you get the wrong laptop you may cause yourself issues with slow applications, short battery life, little connectivity, expensive repairs and devices that aren’t able to support updates.

Small businesses often operate on a budget so you may be tempted to get the cheapest option. However, you may find the downtime, replacement, and support costs could outweigh the initial savings made. Whereas on the other hand you may be spending unnecessarily on high performance options for all members of staff. If most of your roles are using basic functions like email, spreadsheets and web based systems you don’t need to be spending unnecessarily. When purchasing a laptop for your small business start by considering what each member of your team will be doing. Administrative staff will likely be using email, office software, web based tools, video calling and accounting software. Sales staff may want something light weight with good battery life and connectivity on the go. Whereas designers, engineers, developers and video editors may want something with a higher performing processor, increased memory and dedicated graphics. Don’t just go for the same spec. Group your roles into a couple of categories. For example, standard office, mobile and specialist. This will make it easier to purchase the correct laptops for everyone. Check the system requirements for any essential business software and look at what hardware your software providers recommend. Consider how your business may change in the future. You may want to use some new cloud services, handle larger data sets and higher performance software. Ensure your choice provides you with a bit of extra performance to cater for the expected usage over the time between replacements. Don’t get extra that you won’t realistically use.

CPU

The CPU will impact how fast your applications will perform, how well you can multitask and how well it will perform when running high performance applications. Most mainstream CPUs will be fine for general office use. However, there are many different types and models and will affect the performance. Look at the exact model, generation, cores and independent reviews.

For general document creation, web based applications and video calling a modern mainstream CPU will be fine. However, if you have developers, analysts and users of specialist software you may want to consider getting a higher performance option. If you have a heavy workload such as compiling, virtual machines, large spreadsheets or media you want to consider this in more detail. Laptops will also suffer from overheating and power management. Two laptops with the same CPU may perform differently when running long workloads due to the design, cooling and power limitations. Look at reviews for how they will perform when running longer workloads.

RAM & Storage

This will affect how many applications and web pages you can have open without slowing down. If you have a basic office role you may be fine with a lower spec. However, it can be useful to get 16GB for most business users that will be multitasking. For specialist roles 32GB or more may be needed.

Ensure your laptop has upgradable RAM. Some slim laptops will have the RAM soldered onto the motherboard. If you are going to use the laptop for a few years it will be better to get enough RAM from the start. If you have a lot of RAM you won’t have to replace it as early.

Try to get an SSD to ensure quick boot up and app opening times. The size will depend on how much you need to store locally. 512GB would be a sensible option for most staff. However, if you have users that will be using lots of media or large data sets you may need more. Don’t rely on the laptop storage to store your important information. Always use approved cloud storage and managed back up solutions. Ensure you can recover your data.

Security

As your business laptops may contain customer information, financial information, internal documents and company systems you should consider security. Look at what security features are available for the OS, firmware, secure boot, encryption and biometrics.

Ensure your chosen devices can be updated with OS and firmware updates for the expected lifespan. Ensure your security software, identity and device management platforms can be used with your chosen models. If you have a team you may want to get devices that can be centrally managed. This can help with applying policies, setting up, updating and reacting to lost devices.

Ensure you have standard processes in place for user accounts, multi factor authentication, locking screens, approved software and data storage. Your staff should know what to do if the device is lost. Both the hardware and how its set up will impact the security.

Build Quality, Keyboard & Portability

Ensure the laptop is comfortable to use daily and has a good build quality. If your staff are travelling between customer sites a lighter 13/14inch laptop may be better. However, if they are mostly using it from a desk a larger screen may be better. Also consider what the total weight is including the charger and any essential accessories.

Look at the build quality of the hinges, keyboard, touchpad and body. If your staff are typing lots having a well spaced keyboard and backlit keyboard may be beneficial. Ensure the webcam, microphone and speakers are good for regular online meetings. If possible try a sample device. Many business laptops will have good build quality features such as durable bodies, servicing and longer support periods. However, this will depend on the model family. Look at independent reviews for build quality and what the warranty covers.

Ports, Connectivity & External Displays

Small businesses often use their laptops with printers, scanners, projectors, monitors, storage and meeting room equipment. Ensure you know what accessories your staff will be using and that the laptop has the correct ports. Many devices will still use USB-A so it can still be useful. If the USB-C port can be used for data transfer, charging and display then it will be useful. Some roles may need HDMI, Ethernet and memory card slots. Not all USB-C ports will provide display output or high watt charging. Check the manufacturers technical specifications. If your staff are using docking stations ensure they are compatible with your laptop and how many external displays (and resolution) you will need.

Look at what wireless capabilities the laptop has. Ensure it can connect to your business network using the correct Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. If your staff are travelling you may want to ensure they have a reliable mobile hotspot or a model with mobile connectivity.

Battery Life & Charging

If your staff are using the laptop away from their desk, attending meetings around the site or travelling then battery life will be an important factor. The battery life quoted by the manufacturer will be based on specific scenarios and may not give you the full day of video calling and multitasking. Look at independent reviews for the correct workload.

Look at how big the charger is, how quickly it charges and if it can be charged via USB-C. If you have a few models that can be charged with the same charger it will make it easier to have spares. However, ensure the power requirements are met. Decide how your staff will report if the battery is deteriorating and when they will need replacing. As the battery ages it will affect how well it performs. If it is constantly connected to power it may have different requirements than if it is being used regularly on the go. Follow the manufacturers guidelines for charging and storage. Don’t fall for any battery myths.

Warranty, Support & Replacement

If your laptop breaks it can cause some disruption and unexpected costs. Look at how long the warranty lasts, what is covered, where it can be serviced, how quickly it can be repaired, what accidental damage cover is available and if replacement parts are available. Ensure you know if it can be serviced on site and if there is priority support. This may be an extra cost.

Having a few models can make it easier to support your team. Having a few approved models may mean it’s easier to have spare chargers, repairs and standard images. However, don’t push specialist staff into using the wrong hardware. Having an asset register will help with planning replacements. This will include the model, serial number, who it is allocated to, purchase date, end of warranty date and any repair history.

Total Cost of Ownership

When purchasing a laptop you need to consider the total cost of ownership. This includes accessories, licences, security, management, extended warranties, repairs, time to set up and eventual replacement. A cheaper laptop may become slow and unreliable quickly making it more expensive overall. Consider how long you expect to use the device for. Consider the downtime costs and if you need a spare for key roles. Compare the models with the same amount of RAM, storage, screen and warranty. Don’t be fooled by promotional pricing when the spec isn’t the same. Creating a simple spreadsheet to log the price, key specs, warranty, support options and estimated lifetime cost of each option will help you make a clear decision. This can also help with explaining your decision to the business owners or finance team.

Set Up

When purchasing your laptops ensure they are set up. Install any approved OS updates, set up security, add to management tools, install any required applications and ensure the back up and recovery works. Provide your staff with a standard set up guide. This should include passwords, multi factor authentication, updates, data storage and support contacts.

Don’t use one admin account. Each user should have their own account and permissions should be based on their role. Keep track of what devices have been allocated. Ensure you have a secure process in place for returning devices.

Having a standard set up will help to reduce any differences in set up and make it easier to support. This will ensure your laptop is ready to use.

Conclusion

The best laptop for your small business will be one that matches the workload of the employee, has the correct performance and security and can be supported throughout its useful life. Consider the CPU, RAM, SSD, screen, battery, ports, build quality, warranty and total cost of ownership. Using roles will help to ensure you don’t get under powered devices and are not overspending.

Test a sample model, ensure software is compatible and check the support options. Printerworld can be a great place to start looking at your requirements for laptops and printers however ensure you are making your decision based on the exact models, spec and support options you have available.
