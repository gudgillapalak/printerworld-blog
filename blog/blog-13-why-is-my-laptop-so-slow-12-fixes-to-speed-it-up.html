Why Is My Laptop So Slow? 12 Fixes to Speed It Up

Learn twelve practical ways to troubleshoot a slow laptop, from startup apps and storage cleanup to updates, malware checks, cooling and hardware upgrades.

Fixes & repair·8 min read·Guide 13 of 50

Introduction

A laptop can start to get slow over time which can be very frustrating. Applications will take longer to open, you might find your browser getting stuck, your video calls getting choppy and it will take longer to start up. There can be many reasons for a slow laptop. It could be due to too many startup apps, low storage, background processes, malware, overheating or out of date software/hardware.

Before purchasing a new laptop, try following these steps. Firstly save your important files, try to work out when your laptop is running slow, and follow the steps below. Some are simple to change, while others may require a technicians help. These are general steps and the names of the menus may differ according to your OS version. Do not delete files you are not familiar with or use any unknown optimisation tools.

1. Restart

A quick way to start is to simply restart your laptop. This will close any running processes and reset any temporary states. If your laptop has been in 'sleep’ mode for a long time, a full restart can help to improve any temporary issues. Make sure you save any open documents and restart via the restart option within your OS.

After your laptop has restarted, let it complete any start up processes and updates. If your laptop is only running fast for a short period of time, try to work out what applications are open and if any processes are using up resources. Constantly restarting your laptop will not help any hardware or software issues but can help to identify if it is a temporary issue.

2. Reduce Startup Apps

Many applications have startup features that automatically open when your laptop boots up. These will use up RAM, CPU and storage. Having lots of startup apps can slow down your boot time and make your laptop seem busy.

Within Windows check your Startup Apps within Task Manager or Settings. Within Mac check Login Items within System Settings. Un-check any apps that you do not need to open automatically. However, do not uncheck any security programmes or essential device tools. If you are unsure what the application does, search online to find out who published it and what it does.

This will not uninstall the application but you can still open it when required. Occasionally check your startup apps as new ones may be added.

3. Check CPU/RAM/Storage Usage

Open Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (Mac). This will show you what applications are using up your resources. If you have many tabs open in your browser, video editing software, cloud sync or background updates then this could be using up a lot of RAM/CPU.

Sort by CPU, RAM or Storage to see what unexpected applications are using up resources. Un-check any programmes you know you do not need. If you see an application you do not know, do not close it yet. Try to work out if it is part of your OS or a trusted application. Malware can sometimes cause unexpected usage so combine this with a reputable security scan.

If you still have high RAM usage while doing regular tasks then your laptop may not have enough RAM. Adding extra RAM may help if your laptop supports it.

4. Free Up Storage

Having low storage can cause issues with updating, temporary files and applications. Check your storage settings to see what large files, downloads and applications are taking up space. Make sure to empty your recycle bin/trash after checking.

Try to use the storage tools within your OS. Uninstall any applications properly. If possible move your personal media to an external drive or trusted cloud storage. Make sure to backup your files.

Do not delete any system folders/files just because they look large. If you are unsure if you need it, leave it or ask for help. Try to keep your laptop with enough free space to allow updates and regular use.

5. Update OS and Applications

Updating your OS can help to improve performance, compatibility and security. Open your official OS update settings to check for updates. Update from trusted sources and restart when prompted.

Update any key applications and device drivers from your laptop manufacturers support tools or official vendors. Avoid using driver update tools from unknown publishers. Backup any key files before doing a large update and ensure your laptop has sufficient charge or is plugged into the correct adapter.

Your laptop may be busy while it downloads, installs and indexes. Let it complete before assuming it is now running slower.

6. Run a Malware Scan

Malware, adware and unknown browser extensions can affect your performance, show unwanted adverts and use up resources. Run a full scan using a reputable security programme you already have or from a trusted provider. Ensure your definitions and software are up to date.

Check your browser extensions and delete any you do not recognise. Be wary of free downloads, emails with attachments and pop ups stating your laptop is infected. Do not call any numbers shown on unexpected security alerts and do not install remote access software.

If your security tool identifies a severe infection, follow the suggested removal method. Any business devices should be reported to your organisations IT/security team.

7. Optimize Browser

Browsers can take up a lot of RAM. Close any tabs you are not using and delete any extensions that are not providing much benefit. If your browser has a task manager or performance tool, check what page or extension is using too many resources.

Ensure your browser is up to date. If you notice your laptop is running slow on a specific website try using a different browser or open it without any unnecessary extensions. Avoid adding too many security extensions as they can cause more background activity.

If you are doing research, use bookmarks or reading lists rather than having dozens of tabs open. This small change can make a big difference on a low RAM laptop.

8. Check for Overheating

Overheating can cause performance issues as your laptop may reduce the CPU speed to protect itself. Common overheating symptoms include a noisy fan, hot bottom, lagging when doing intensive tasks or shutting down unexpectedly. This could be due to blocked vents, dust, a faulty fan or high background activity.

Place your laptop on a firm flat surface and ensure vents are clear. Avoid placing on soft surfaces such as bedding. Close any unnecessary intensive applications and check if the fan is quieter. If it is still overheating during light tasks, get it checked by a professional. Any internal cleaning and thermal work should be done using the correct tools.

If you notice any burning smells, excessive heat or constant shutdowns, stop using your laptop. If there are signs of electrical damage or a swollen battery, get help from a qualified person.

9. Change Power Settings

Some laptops have power saving modes which will reduce the CPU performance to save battery life. If you are noticing your laptop running slow when not plugged in, check what power mode you are in. Compare how it runs when plugged into the correct charger. Change to a balance or performance mode. This can increase the heat, fan noise and battery use.

Check your manufacturers power management application for specific settings. Do not change any advanced CPU or firmware settings unless you know what you are doing. Your laptop may also run slower if there is an issue with the battery or charger.

10. Check Storage Health

A faulty storage device can cause long load times, freezing, error messages and issues starting your OS. If your laptop is making strange clicking/grinding noises and has a mechanical hard drive, backup your key files immediately. Modern SSDs do not have any moving parts but can still fail.

Check your storage health using your manufacturers storage health tool or trusted OS diagnostic tool. If your system is showing any errors or your drive is disappearing, avoid writing unnecessary data and get help from a professional. Constantly trying to repair a faulty drive may not help protect your data.

If your drive is in good health but is an older mechanical HDD, upgrading to a compatible SSD may help improve performance. Check your laptops interface and upgrade requirements.

11. Upgrade RAM/SSD

If your laptop is constantly running out of RAM/storage then upgrading your hardware may help more than adjusting settings. Upgrading from a mechanical hard drive to an SSD can help improve boot times and application opens. Increasing your RAM can help with multi-tasking if you do not have enough.

Not all laptops can be upgraded. Some laptops have soldered RAM/storage which can be difficult to upgrade. Check your service manual and exact model specs. Consider the cost of parts and labour. A technician can check if it is compatible and if it would be worth upgrading.

Upgrading will not help with all issues causing your laptop to run slow. If your processor is not powerful enough for the software you need or there is another hardware issue then you may not notice much of an improvement.

12. Reinstall/Reset

If your laptop is still having software issues, you may want to consider resetting or reinstalling. This is a big step as depending on what you choose, it can remove applications, settings and personal files. Backup your documents, photos, licence details and any files outside of your synced folders. Ensure you can access your backup

Use your official OS recovery tool. Follow your manufacturers guidance. If your laptop belongs to your employer/education, contact your IT administrator. Do not use a reset to avoid finding out if there is an issue with your drive, battery or overheating.

After reinstalling, ensure you update and install essential drivers from an official source. Carefully restore your files. Only install the applications you need.

When to get help from a professional

If your laptop is constantly freezing, shutting down, overheating, showing hardware errors or signs of a faulty storage device then get a technician to help. They can test your RAM, storage, cooling, battery and power. This is especially important if you have valuable files on your laptop or it is used for business critical tasks.

Before getting your laptop serviced, note when it is running slow, what has changed recently and what you have tried. If possible backup your data. Let them know if it is under warranty. This can help identify if it would be best value to repair, upgrade or replace.

Conclusion

There can be many reasons why your laptop is running slowly. Follow the steps above to try and identify the issue. Restart your laptop, reduce startup apps, check resource usage, free up storage, update software, run a malware scan, optimize your browser, check for overheating, check power settings, check storage health and consider upgrading RAM/SSD. A reset should be a last resort with a reliable backup.

If your laptop is still running poorly or you are noticing warning signs of a hardware failure, get it diagnosed by a professional. The correct solution will depend on your laptops model, condition and what it is used for.
