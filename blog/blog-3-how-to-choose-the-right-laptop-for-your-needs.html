How to Choose the Right Laptop for Your Needs

A practical laptop buying guide covering purpose, processor, RAM, storage, display, battery, portability, connectivity and budget.

Buying guides·8 min read·Guide 3 of 50

Introduction

Purchasing a laptop is a big commitment and it can become a valuable tool in your daily routine. Whether you're a student using your laptop for classes, assignments or research, or a professional using it for documents, meetings and specialist software – or even a creative needing a powerful processor, memory and graphics – you want a device that will fit your needs. However, there is quite a lot to think about when looking at the specs of a laptop. It can become overwhelming deciding between different models with different processors, amounts of RAM, storage, screen and battery. The best laptop for you won't necessarily be the most expensive one or the one with the longest list of specs. Rather it should be one that will easily handle your tasks, fit within your budget and will be able to get support.

To help you decide:

1. Tasks

Write down what you will be using your laptop for. If you're just using it for basic tasks such as browsing, emailing, video calls and word processing then an entry level or mid range laptop might be fine. If you're a student using office software, learning platforms and research tools then you'll generally benefit from having a responsive processor, sufficient memory and an SSD.

If you're going to be using it for programming, data analysis or multitasking then you may want to consider a more powerful processor and more RAM. As a developer you should look into the requirements of your IDE, virtual machines, containers and any other tools you use. If you're using it for graphic design, video editing, 3D or gaming you might need a graphics card, colourful display and more memory.

If you're using specialist software for your profession then always check the official requirements for that software. Whilst you may be able to run the software on a laptop that meets the minimum requirements, it will run much better if it meets the recommended specs. Don't waste money on a high spec gaming laptop if you won't actually need it for your everyday tasks.

2. Processor

The processor (CPU) is responsible for running general computing tasks. The performance of the processor will affect how quickly your applications run and how well it will handle high intensity tasks. It's a good idea to look at the family and generation of the processor. Modern entry and mid range processors can be very capable for everyday use. However, if you're doing more intensive tasks then you might want to look for a higher performance range of processors with more cores and a better sustained performance. You should also consider the energy efficiency of the processor, as this will affect how hot it gets, how noisy the fan will be and how long the battery will last. Try to look up unbiased reviews for that exact spec of laptop. Even if two laptops have similar sounding processors they can have different performances due to the cooling solution, power and memory. Sometimes a laptop with a well designed cooling solution can have a better sustained performance than a slim laptop with a faster processor.

3. RAM

RAM is used as a short term memory for your applications and OS. If your laptop doesn't have lots of RAM available it can cause it to use more from your storage, meaning you may experience a laggy performance when trying to multitask. If you're using lots of open tabs in your web browser along with office software, communication tools and other tools then you may benefit from more RAM.For basic tasks a low spec of RAM may be fine. However, if you're doing more intensive tasks or using modern applications then 8GB can feel tight. Many users will find it more comfortable to have 16GB RAM to allow them to run their general productivity, studying or development tasks. If you're a creative professional or using virtual machines then you might need 32GB or more depending on the software you're using. Check if the RAM is upgradable or soldered to the motherboard. If it's soldered then you may be stuck with that amount of RAM. Choose an amount that will suit your future needs.

4. SSD Storage

Most new laptops will be best considered with a solid state drive (SSD). This will give you faster access to your OS, applications and files. This can help speed up your boot time, application loading and overall performance. You'll also want to consider the size of your storage. If you're using it for light use and storing your files in the cloud then 256GB may be enough. However, once you start installing applications, media and project files it can fill up quickly. A 512GB SSD will be more flexible for many users. If you're working with large video, design or research files then you may need 1TB or more. Or have a reliable backup plan for external storage.

Check if your laptop has upgradable storage. Don't forget to back up your data.

5. Screen Size & Display

Laptops come in various sizes. Smaller laptops are easier to carry around but larger screens can give you more space to work with. If you're travelling a lot then a 13" or 14" laptop can give you a good balance. If you're mostly using your laptop at your desk then a 15" or 16" may be a better option.

The resolution, brightness, viewing angles, colours, refresh rate and finish will all affect the performance of your screen. A full HD screen will be a good starting point for many users. Creatives should consider the colours more. If you're working outdoors or in bright rooms then you may need a brighter screen with an anti glare finishIf possible try to view the screen in person. Check how comfortable the text looks at your preferred scaling and if the hinge and viewing angles are comfortable.

6. Battery Life

The battery life will depend on the battery size, processor, screen, software, tasks and settings. The advertised battery life from the manufacturer will usually be based on testing in controlled environments. This can vary greatly when using your laptop for video calls, high screen brightness, gaming and high intensity applications.

Look for reviews that have tested the battery life in realistic situations. Check if the laptop can be charged using USB-C. Also check how many watts the adapter is and if it can be charged using a compatible power bank. A fast charging feature can be useful but shouldn't be the main factor.

If you're a student or travelling professional then the weight and size of the charger can be very important. Even if it has a great battery life it may be inconvenient if the power adapter is unusually heavy.

7. Keyboard, Touchpad, Webcam & Sound

Whilst looking at the specs it's also important to consider the keyboard. If you're typing a lot then having a comfortable keyboard with well spaced keys and useful travel can make a big difference. Check if the keyboard is well laid out, has a good layout for function keys and has a backlit keyboard. You'll also want a responsive and accurate touchpad. If you're using your laptop for online classes or meetings then you should look at the webcam resolution, microphone quality and speakers. Having noise cancellation and a good webcam position can make you look more professional whilst on calls. If you're using a headset or external mic then check if there are enough ports.

The build quality can also be important. Ensure it has a sturdy hinge, solid keyboard and feels like it's made from the right materials for the intended use. If you're using it daily then it should be designed to handle normal use.

8. Ports, Connectivity & Compatibility

Work out what devices you'll be connecting to your laptop. External monitors, USB drives, printers, projectors, memory cards, wired network and sound devices. Check how many ports there are and what type. USB-A, USB-C, HDMI, Audio and Ethernet if needed. Some slim laptops may only have a few ports so you may need to invest in a hub or docking station.

Check the laptop has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Also ensure it's compatible with your OS. Check if your printer or other peripherals have the correct drivers. If you're planning on using two external monitors then ensure the laptop can output to multiple displays.

9. Set a Budget & Value for Money

Before browsing set a budget. Work out what you can't compromise on. Look at laptops with similar amounts of RAM, Storage and Screen. The advertised price may not be for the same spec as in the picture.

Consider how long the warranty lasts, how easy it is to get to a service centre, if it has accidental damage cover, how much it will cost to upgrade and if the essential accessories are included. A slightly higher priced laptop with a good build quality and local support may be a better value for money in the long run.

Don't waste money on features you won't be using. Invest in the parts that will directly affect your work.

Conclusion

Understanding what you'll be using your laptop for will help you pick the right one for you. Ensure it has the right processor, RAM, storage, screen, battery and connectivity for your most used tasks. Check the exact spec, warranty and if it can be upgraded. Try to look up reviews for that specific model.

A good laptop should be suitable for your current needs without becoming frustrating. We can help our customers consider what laptop would be best for them alongside their printer and accessory needs. Ensure the laptop you're looking at has the right specs, is available and has good after sales support.

Think about the lifespan

When choosing your laptop you should think about how long you would expect to use it. Check what warranty the manufacturer is offering. How easy it is to get to an authorised service. How much common parts such as a battery, keyboard and charger would cost to replace. If it has upgradable storage it can be easier to upgrade as your files increase. However, if it's a slim laptop with soldered memory you may still find it suitable if you choose enough RAM. Ensure the OS will be supported for long enough. Also ensure it meets the requirements of any applications you're likely to use in the coming years. If you need it to be portable then consider the actual weight of the laptop and power adapter. A comfortable keyboard, sturdy hinge and good webcam can make you happier using your laptop daily.
