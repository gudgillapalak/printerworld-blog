Why Regular Printer Servicing Is Important for Businesses

Understand why regular business printer servicing reduces downtime, improves print quality, controls costs and supports a productive workplace.

Care & tips·8 min read·Guide 6 of 50

Introduction

Printers are still very much used in many businesses. Team members will use printers to print important documents like invoices, contracts, reports, delivery documents, labels, presentations and customer records. If you have a printer that is not functioning properly, staff may be wasting time trying to fix the issue. This can cause delays to key documentation and cause disruption to customer facing tasks. If the printer is not looked after, you may start to see a loss in productivity for your business.

Regular printer maintenance allows you to service your printer. This means you can check the printer, ensure it is cleaned and the correct parts are in stock. You can check for wear and tear and fix any small issues. Depending on the printer type, age, usage, environment and manufacturers advice you will need to service your printer regularly. If you use your printer regularly throughout the day, regular maintenance can be more practical.

1. Reduce downtime

Issues with your printer are rarely convenient. If your printer is having an issue feeding paper, causing problems with your toner or causing network issues whilst you are busy during month end invoicing, this will cause a delay to multiple people. If you only have one printer in your business, a short downtime can cause a queue to build up.

By having a regular maintenance schedule, you can get a technician to check your printer. You can ensure paper feed rollers, separation pads, accessible areas, sensors and other parts are checked. This will allow you to identify any early signs of wear and tear. Whilst not all issues can be avoided, having a service history will allow you to identify any regular issues. Having your printer serviced regularly will allow you to ensure you have the correct consumables in stock. You will also be able to plan to have support in place ahead of a known busy time. You can also consider if you need an extra printer or print solution for key workflows.

2. Improve print quality

Having poor print quality can cause you to look unprofessional. Poor print quality can cause issues with faded text, streaks, spots, inconsistent colours, smudging and repeating lines. This can cause issues with reading documents. All important documents such as invoices, forms, proposals and labels should have good quality and consistency.

There can be multiple reasons why print quality is poor. Inkjet printers may need a check of nozzles, alignment and cleaning as advised by the manufacturer. Laser printers may need a check of toner, drum units, transfer, rollers and fuser. Issues can also be caused by incorrect paper, incorrect media settings and poor quality consumables.

A service professional can check using test pages and diagnostics. You don’t want to be replacing parts unnecessarily so you want to identify the root cause. If you keep samples of poor print you can also identify the issue quicker. Note if it is affecting all pages, just colour or certain papers.

3. Reduce total printing costs

When purchasing a printer there are many costs to consider. Along with the initial purchase price you will also be paying for ink/toner, drums (if applicable), paper, parts, electricity, repair and staff time. If your printer is not looked after you may be causing wastage due to repeated cleaning, paper jams, test prints and needing to reprint. Having your printer serviced can help identify any issues that may be increasing your running costs. Worn rollers can cause repeated paper jams. Incorrect settings can cause colour printing unnecessarily. If your printer is not set up for double sided printing you may be using more paper than needed. If your printer isn’t suited to the volume you print each month you may have frequent service interruptions or need to replace your printer earlier than expected.

You should consider the cost per page, consumable yield and volume you actually print each month. If you have a department that prints a lot you may want to invest in a model that can handle increased volumes. Your service provider can help you decide if it is more cost effective to repair, replace, adjust settings or change the type of printer. Consider the age of the device, estimated repair cost, availability of parts and expected future volume.

4. Increase lifespan

Printers have lots of moving parts and will naturally suffer from wear and tear. Paper rollers spin, trays open and close and internal parts can cause heat, pressure and mechanical movement. Dust and paper can build up especially if your printer is used frequently. If not looked after properly, small issues can cause frequent paper jams, inconsistent feeding and unnecessary stress on the device.

Preventative maintenance will focus on the areas as advised by the manufacturer. This can include cleaning the correct accessible areas, checking paper path, service messages, rollers and ensuring your printer is operating within it’s intended volume. Laser printers can have a range of maintenance parts such as drums and fusers. These will have a service life depending on the model. Inkjet printers have different requirements, especially for printheads.

Not all printers will need servicing at the same frequency. If you have a printer that isn’t used much in your home office you may need to service it less frequently than a multi-functional printer for your whole department. Follow the manufacturers advice and consider the volume you print each month, paper quality and any previous issues. Do not open any covers or touch any hot or sensitive parts unless stated in the manual.

5. Improve productivity in the workplace

If you have a printer that is shared amongst staff members they will have to leave their desk to find another printer, reprint and/or contact IT. If you have frequent issues this can cause a lot of interruptions.

Having a reliable printer will improve your workflow. Staff members can print when needed, departments can meet deadlines and your IT team can spend their time on high value tasks instead of fixing the same printer issues. Having your printer set up correctly can also help you with secure printing, network access, scanning to approved locations and efficient double sided printing.

Consider how you have your printers set up. If your printer isn’t in a convenient location for your teams you may have unnecessary movement. If too many people are using your printer it could be overworked. If you have clear guidelines for paper loading, tray selection and error reporting this will help stop any unnecessary misuse. Printer servicing will work best if you have them in a logical location, with clear guidelines and correct setup.

6. Help with security and reliable connectivity

Printers are networked devices and may contain information about your print jobs, address book, network details and more. Using a service will help you to check your printer has the correct firmware and configuration. Ensure you have strong admin details, limit unnecessary access and follow your IT team’s network security policy.

Sometimes issues with your printer can be caused by connectivity issues. Your printer may be in good condition but having issues accessing it due to the IP address changing, driver issues, wireless or print queue issues. Your service provider or IT admin can check your connectivity, drivers and access. Ensure firmware updates are coming directly from the manufacturer and are carried out as per the official guidelines. Take the correct precautions to avoid disruption.

Before your technician accesses your network printer ensure you have clarity on what level of access is required and follow your internal processes. Avoid showing any passwords or sensitive document details in your service history. Agree who is responsible for security.

7. Create a printer maintenance schedule

Your printer maintenance schedule doesn’t need to be complicated. You should start by noting the printer model, serial number, location, date installed and approximate monthly volume. Note what consumables the printer uses, warranty status and any regular error messages. This will help you to understand which printers are key to your business and which are suitable for replacement.

Create a simple schedule based on the manufacturers advice and actual usage. Simple checks can be done by staff members to ensure paper is loaded correctly, area is clean and any regular errors reported. Any technical work should be carried out by an authorised or qualified service professional. Keep track of when your printer was serviced, what parts were replaced, what consumables were used and any issues noted. Ensure you have a process in place for urgent issues. Ensure your staff know who to contact, what information to provide and if they need to reprint on another printer. Consider having a backup device for critical printing. Regularly review your schedule as your business grows and printing changes.

Choosing a printer service

A good service provider will be able to explain what checks they will carry out, what the likely costs will be, what parts will be required and how long it will take. Ask if they can service your exact model, if they have spare parts and what warranty they offer on completed work. If you have multiple devices ask if they can keep track of your assets and service history.

Ensure they communicate well. Before approving any repairs ask for a diagnosis and estimate. Ensure you know if the estimate includes labour, parts, delivery and tax. Ask what will happen if further issues are found. Ensure you understand if they will need to have access to data, network or security. Don’t always go for the cheapest quote. Consider technical ability, quick response, transparency, quality of parts and their ability to support your operational needs. Conclusion

Regular printer servicing can help to reduce unnecessary downtime, ensure you have clear print quality, control your running costs and ensure your printer is in good working condition. It can also help to improve productivity by reducing frequent interruptions. You will have better information to plan for repairs and replacements.

Having a printer maintenance schedule as advised by the manufacturer, using your printer sensibly, using the correct consumables, having a secure setup and a clear support process will give you the best results. Identify which printers are key to your daily operations, note how they are used and plan to have them checked based on their volume. Having a planned schedule is usually easier to manage than waiting for your printer to cause issues.
